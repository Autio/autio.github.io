Graph curvature · stage-three research draft

Sharp Hall localization

Novelty and independent human review remain open

A large transport problem can sometimes be replaced by one small matching. This draft studies when missing-road structure makes that possible, and exactly where a local obstruction disappears.

The idea, without the notation

Imagine moving sand from the neighbors of one place in a network to the neighbors of the next place. Each road traveled costs one unit. The cheapest move tells us the curvature of the road between those two places.

Here, any two places share at most k missing neighbors. That restricts where a shortage of direct routes can hide. Above a sharp graph-size cutoff, the difficult part is small. Even the routes that cost three steps line up around one source or one destination.

One ordinary matching then identifies the decisive shortage. A small extra test tells us whether the three-step routes add another cost. This avoids listing the many common-neighbor rows in the larger transport calculation.

The earlier interactive curvature demo introduces the sand-moving idea.

What this revision proposes

  • A quantitative cutoff: a transient Hall obstruction has scaled size at most k² + k + 2 − n. For k ≥ 3 the last possible graph order is exactly k² + k + 1. For k = 2 no such transient witness exists.
  • At that boundary, two numerical witness types survive. The curvature deficit is exactly 1/(ab), and adding one universally connected vertex repairs equality. This does not classify the entire graph up to isomorphism.
  • The large-order matching method handles three-step transport using one canonical matching. The codegree-two argument removes the apparent eight-vertex exception.
  • At codegree two, an explicit all-orders formula classifies every defective edge. Its small local pattern has deficit 1/(ab), doubled precisely when the exceptional source is pendant.

The implemented hybrid has an O(k⁵n²) word-operation bound. The large-order branch takes O(k³n²). A theoretical O(k⁴n²) all-orders version substitutes a classical AOST flow backend at small order; that backend is unimplemented.

Assumptions and interpretation

G is a connected simple graph; its complement has at most k common neighbors at every distinct pair. Curvature uses uniform open-neighborhood measures at idleness zero. At an oriented edge, a ≤ b are endpoint degrees.

The bounds use indexed word arithmetic and unreduced rational output. They do not treat arbitrary-precision normalized Python fractions as unit-cost operations. No optimal or best-known dependence on k is claimed.

The exact propositions, proofs, exceptional cases and prior-work discussion are in the paper. Tests and partial formalization support correctness; they cannot establish novelty.

Close predecessors, now credited

Chen, Liu and You already provide the complement framework and important counting arguments. Our persistent extremal example is exactly their Section 6 construction. The sharp-cutoff example modifies that same template. Their unmodified construction already has a transient zero-idleness defect just below our cutoff.

Li and Lu give unequal-degree weighted Hall criteria and a three-step row bottleneck in a related curvature problem. Asai and Yamada study zero-idleness equality for particular edge-deleted complete graphs. Hall's theorem, matching, transport duality and the flow algorithms are established tools.

The proposed additions are the sharp quantitative bound, boundary refinement and complete localized single-matching reduction. Our search has not established priority. Read the expanded priority review.

Evidence, including the negative results

The final optional backend agrees with independent exact transport on 164,594 eligible edges in all 27,475 connected labeled graphs through six vertices; it deliberately skips 53,900 ineligible edges. A separately coded star audit checks 35,072 cases. The all-orders wrapper passes 5,659 edges, including fallback calls. These protocols overlap and are not added as independent graph coverage.

Fifteen substantive Lean modules compile with Lean and Mathlib 4.19.0. They check actual graph counting and canonical maximizers, weighted Hall and real/integer unit-baseline feasibility, boundary paths and the four-codegree cover. Restricted-set correction arithmetic has an explicit margin hypothesis. Metric cancellation, general higher-cost integrality, the complete algorithm and its runtime are outside an end-to-end formal proof.

Five-trial comparisons include NetworkX, SciPy and POT, with standard reductions already applied to the baselines. POT wins all ten generic transport-matrix medians. Structural matching can help dense all-edge workloads, while cold indexing loses badly on small batches and sparse graphs.

A literal connection to adjunctions

The transport inequalities have two potential-tightening maps, formed by minima and maxima, that make a monotone adjunction. The appendix records that elementary order-theoretic connection. It is established mathematics and gives no runtime improvement by itself.
