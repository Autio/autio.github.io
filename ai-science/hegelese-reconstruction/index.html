Experiment 04 · Mathematics and language design

From checked change to necessary loss

Research note · proofs and executable experiments

Hegelese checks what a proposed change preserves and computes what it loses. This research asks a further question: which losses are forced by a reconstruction demand, and when does that demand leave several incomparable choices?

The first answer to the research question

For a finite deterministic process, consider several views, each of which represents every state declared in that view. The views may be independent, or they may share one protected common overlap. We allow the process and the views to be quotiented, and demand exact reconstruction from the resulting views.

A universal least-loss quotient exists exactly when every compatible tuple of original view states is already realized by a global state. Under these hypotheses, this is also exactly when intersections of feasible quotient kernels remain feasible.

When the condition holds, the universal quotient preserves all view states and merges precisely the global states with identical joint views. When it fails, the quotients that retain different individual views expose the obstruction to a universal choice.

The proof applies to arbitrary deterministic transitions with a common input alphabet and compatible initial states. For shared-overlap views, each view maps onto the common overlap, which remains fixed. Additional protected observations or permissions that rule out the competing quotients need a separate analysis.

No universal least-loss choice. Keeping A and collapsing B works; keeping B and collapsing A also works. The two choices are incomparable. Their common refinement retains both views and still misses two combinations.

These are three fixed examples from the exact finite enumeration. The diagonal example also supports an input that flips both bits; the three-corner example supports a reset to zero. A missing universal choice does not mean that no qualifying quotient exists.

What this adds, and what is classical

Reconstruction by compatible views is closely related to the classical lossless join condition in relational databases. The quotient and factorization arguments are also established mathematics. The notes credit Fagin, Mendelzon and Ullman, Rutten, and the comparison with complete abstractions.

The result of this investigation is a precise criterion for the proposed Hegelese interface, including proofs, examples, and limits. It is not presented as a new discovery of lossless joins or a newly established general principle of abstraction. The separate binary-cut preprint has its own unresolved priority assessment.

Even one arrow can obstruct uniqueness. Two source states map into a three-state observation space. Exact reconstruction requires merging the unused observation with a represented one, but either attachment works. The complete analysis of this shape explains when a universal quotient exists, and why its classification with identity transitions must not be applied unchanged to arbitrary dynamics.

Making criticism consequential

The Hegelian motivation is to make the content and limitations of a representation constrain what follows. A failed proposal supplies more useful content when its obstruction explains an entire class of failures. A successful change supplies stronger evidence when its retained distinctions are characterized universally.

This remains conditional on the source, observations, admissible transformations, and demand. The mathematics also identifies cases where those conditions do not determine one successor. That ambiguity is part of the result. A proof that a loss is unavoidable does not authorize accepting it.

The connection note also examines revision history. A later deterministic operation cannot recover information already erased from its input. A repair across installed representations must identify the retained residual information, original state, or external evidence that makes recovery possible. Hegelese's current lineage protocol works against a fixed source; migration across changing sources is a further design problem.

Proofs, checks and publication

The follow-up computations enumerate 327 two-view relations, 193 three-view relations, 194 cases with repeated joint fibers, 121 arrow maps up to source-state permutation, and 49 cases over a protected common overlap. Independent full searches examine 305,184 congruence tuples. Six further examples specify actual process transitions. Every recorded assertion passes.

The original bridge and the follow-up are proposed for experiments/reconstruction/ in Hegelese through pull request #4.
