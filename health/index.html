Summary action points

Personal suggestions on what actions to take if diagnosed with cancer. Not medical advice.

  • Incorporate weekly fasting into your routine, or a fasting-mimicking diet.
  • Try 24-hour water fasting. Practice water fasting for 24 hours before and after a chemo drip. This has both major protective benefits and can boost the efficacy of the medication.
  • Work to maintain a plant-heavy ketogenic diet to put cancer cells under metabolic stress.
  • Create a supplement protocol with a view towards validated, peer-reviewed effects especially to protect your immune system function.
  • Advocate for yourself to ensure the latest science is available for your specific diagnosis. If feasible, ask for an extended genetic panel of your tumour sample. Look for immunotherapeutic options, and latest trials. Your oncologist needs to worry about many different types of cancer. You only need to worry about one very specific diagnosis.
  • If possible, find someone who can do advocacy on your behalf if dealing with the cancer makes it difficult to do so yourself.
  • Explore whether there are effective repurposed medications that can help weaken or eliminate cancer cells with minimal or manageable side effects. For example: Artemisinin, LDN, Fenbendazole
  • Maintain a social life and seek out the methods that work for you to keep a hopeful mindset.

Diet and Cancer: Fasting, Ketosis, and Metabolic Approaches

A plant-heavy diet, combined with therapeutic ketosis, and especially periods of fasting, carries very many benefits, and I would without hesitation work to adopt these if I had to go through any cancer process again, as long as cachexia is not an active and prominent risk.

Fasting significantly boosts immune function and puts strain on cancer cells. Moreover, water fasting (putting the body into a state of starvation) before and during chemo significantly reduced my chemo side effects (kept my hair, as people always ask, etc). This starvation state protects healthy cells as they then tend to pass new substances, like chemo, elsewhere into the body that might need it more. Cancer cells are always hungry, so absorb even more of the poison. Further, not having to deal with digestion liberates a substantial portion of the immune system to do other things, such as fight cancer. It upregulates autophagy which repairs and recycles the body, and also swiftly takes away the building blocks left over by dying cancer cells so they cannot be used by remaining cancer cells in their growth process.

A ketogenic diet has been established to have anti-cancer effects in numerous types of cancer. Eliminating sugar is a good but not sufficient step to hurt cancer. Glucose is the favourite, though not only, fuel of cancer cells. Many people report adopting a protocol of dietary and supplementation interventions has yielded strong positive results. The general line of thinking seems to be that if you block sufficiently many metabolic pathways for your specific type of cancer, then the cells inevitably weaken and either die or are beatable by your immune system, ferroptosis, etc. See: Starving cancer.

Supplements: Finding What Works

Supplements can make a meaningful difference in cancer treatment, but professional guidance is valuable in finding actually useful combinations. It's important to remember that supplements, at best, supplement proper medical treatment - they don't replace it.

In my experience, supplementation has been effective in several ways:

  • Boosting immune system function
  • Remedying deficiencies
  • Enhancing the efficacy of chemotherapy and other medications
  • Directly promoting cancer cell death (apoptosis)

All of these elements have been beneficial in my case, but particularly maintaining a strong immune system which enabled immunotherapy to function more effectively.

Navigating the world of supplements is challenging. There are many bad incentives to sell products that are ineffective or potentially harmful. Additionally, gold-standard scientific research with double-blind trials is difficult to conduct for supplement combinations, even when the causal pathways seem justified. Testing multiple supplement combinations requires exponentially more complex and resource-intensive trials, while offering limited profit potential.

My approach has been to understand the proposed biological mechanisms for each supplement and weigh them against known or potential side effects. I've documented my personal protocol in this spreadsheet. You can find many other protocols shared by others who have overcome difficult prognoses. I strongly recommend studying the background of any supplement you consider taking, or validating them with a holistic nurse. I've found Heal Navigator invaluable for this guidance.

Based on my research and experience, I would strongly recommend three supplements in particular:

  • Liposomal vitamin C
  • Vitamin D
  • Melatonin

I haven't found substantial evidence of significant adverse effects for any of these. Regarding melatonin specifically, I know people who regularly take 500 times the standard daily dose. There's an important correlation between melatonin and thymus function, with the thymus being critical for T-cell function and immune response.
