Ignition

Nothing on the internet cures ADHD.
This page knows that.

ADHD is a performance problem, not a knowledge problem — you already know what to do. The deficit lives at the moment of doing. So this page doesn't explain anything. It lends you a prefrontal cortex for the next 25 minutes: it holds your working memory, externalizes time, and shrinks the task until starting is trivial. That's the whole trick. Why each step works — the actual evidence

Dump — offloading working memory

Barkley's model frames ADHD as a deficit of self-directed executive functions — including nonverbal working memory. The corollary is his most-quoted clinical rule: externalize everything. Information held in the world (a list on a screen) doesn't decay the way information held in an ADHD working memory does. Writing unresolved tasks down also measurably reduces their intrusiveness (unfinished tasks otherwise keep pinging attention — the Zeigarnik effect).

Barkley, R. A. (1997). Behavioral inhibition, sustained attention, and executive functions. Psychological Bulletin, 121(1). · Masicampo & Baumeister (2011), JPSP — writing plans for unfulfilled goals eliminates their interference with other tasks.
Pick one — choice paralysis is an initiation tax

ADHD impairs prioritization far more than execution, and every open option keeps competing for attention. Committing to a single item converts an open-ended decision problem (expensive for a weak central executive) into a closed motor problem (cheap). The pick doesn't need to be optimal — task initiation rate, not selection quality, is what predicts output in ADHD samples.

See executive-function accounts of ADHD decision-making, e.g., Willcutt et al. (2005), Biological Psychiatry — meta-analysis of EF deficits.
Shrink + if-then — implementation intentions

The single best-replicated behavioral trick in this page. An implementation intention ("when situation X, I will do Y") delegates initiation from effortful deliberation to automatic cue-response — precisely the handoff an ADHD brain needs. Gollwitzer & Sheeran's meta-analysis across 94 studies found d ≈ 0.65 on goal attainment; Gawrilow & Gollwitzer showed the effect holds in children with ADHD, where if-then plans normalized response inhibition to control levels.

Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006), Advances in Experimental Social Psychology, 38. · Gawrilow & Gollwitzer (2008), Cognitive Therapy and Research, 32.
The shrinking disc — externalizing time

Time blindness is not a metaphor: ADHD reliably impairs time reproduction and estimation (deficits scale with interval length). A number on a clock is symbolic; a red area physically disappearing is perceptual. Perceptual time doesn't need working memory to track. This is why the analog Time-Timer design became a de facto standard in ADHD clinical practice.

Barkley, Edwards, Laneri, Fletcher & Metevia (2001), Journal of Abnormal Child Psychology — time reproduction deficits in ADHD. · Toplak, Dockstader & Tannock (2006), Journal of Neuroscience Methods — review of temporal information processing in ADHD.
Chime + streak — immediate reward for a steep discounter

ADHD brains discount delayed rewards more steeply than controls (a meta-analytically robust finding), which is why "it'll pay off next month" motivates approximately nothing. The fix isn't discipline; it's moving the reward to now. A completion chime, a counter that visibly increments, a streak you'd feel losing — small, immediate, contingent. It's the same mechanism behind every slot machine, pointed at your tax return.

Jackson & MacKillop (2016), Biological Psychiatry: CNNI — meta-analysis of delay discounting in ADHD. · Volkow et al. (2009), JAMA — reduced dopamine reward-pathway markers in adult ADHD.
The 5-minute movement break — acute exercise

Single bouts of moderate aerobic exercise produce measurable short-term improvements in inhibition and attention in ADHD — one of the few non-pharmacological effects that survives meta-analysis. A brisk walk between sprints is not a break from the protocol; it is the protocol.

Cerrillo-Urbina et al. (2015), Child: Care, Health and Development — meta-analysis of exercise effects in ADHD. · Pontifex et al. (2013), Journal of Pediatrics — acute exercise improves inhibitory control in ADHD.
What's deliberately missing

No gamified forest, no XP, no settings page, no account. Every configurable option is a fresh decision, and decisions are the tax this page exists to eliminate. Body doubling (working alongside another person) has promising but still mostly anecdotal evidence — if it works for you, a video call with a friend on mute does the job better than any app. And nothing here replaces the boring heavy hitters above.
