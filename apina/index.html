The advice-to-flow hypothesis

A prospective research system for event-driven, AI-mediated investor attention

Research white paper · Version 0.1 · 28 October 2026

Status: infrastructure and hypothesis formation. No demonstrated alpha. No live trading.

We do not need to predict what an ideal investor should do. We need to estimate what advice real investors will encounter, which actions it makes salient, and whether the resulting demand remains unpriced when we can act.

Abstract

Large public events reorganize investor attention. An increasing share of that attention may pass through a small number of AI assistants. If those assistants repeatedly surface the same securities, funds, and narratives, they could concentrate investment demand. APINA CAPITAL investigates whether event-induced changes in AI recommendations predict subsequent market activity and returns beyond public news, sector exposure, and momentum.

The system collects public evidence, samples matched hypothetical investor conversations, records recommendation distributions, and builds a longitudinal dataset on IMT. Local inference uses the machine's NVIDIA RTX 5090 through its existing model service. The operational database preserves raw observations and their availability times; consistent, verified snapshots are backed up to Google Drive. The public paper describes the method and its development without publishing private runtime configuration, raw model transcripts, or financial credentials.

There is no recruited human panel. Consequently, the conversion from advice to actual trades is not directly identified. We represent it as an interval of assumptions and test reduced-form predictions against observed public market data. Local models are research proxies. They are never labeled as direct observations of ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, or Grok. Only authentic, recorded samples from those products or their identified APIs can populate their respective channels.

The research question is falsifiable. If recommendation changes add no predictive value beyond simpler baselines after realistic costs and timestamp constraints, the proposed AI-specific edge is rejected. An event-driven strategy discovered along the way must be evaluated under its actual mechanism.

1. The opportunity and the missing link

The proposed sequence is:

Public event → investment questions → AI recommendations → investor actions → market demand → price response.

Evidence exists for parts of this sequence. Barber and Odean document attention-based retail buying. Research on investment recommendations from language models reports systematic product preferences. The BIS identifies common models and decision rules as a possible source of correlated behavior. These results motivate investigation; none establishes profitable trading ahead of AI-mediated retail demand.[1–3]

The missing link is the magnitude, timing, and surprise of the induced demand. A frequently recommended security may be too liquid to respond materially. Its expected popularity may already be reflected in price. A less prominent security may receive demand through an ETF rather than through direct recommendations. An answer may recommend doing nothing.

APINA therefore predicts three distinct objects:

  1. Event-induced recommendation exposure.
  2. A sensitivity range for incremental demand.
  3. Returns available after a decision could actually have been made, net of costs and matched exposures.

An observed mention, simulated purchase, estimated flow, and realized return are different data types. The database and reports preserve these distinctions.

2. Research population and observable channels

The target population is self-directed investors who consult AI assistants after salient events. The initial scope is U.S.-listed liquid equities and ETFs, with explicit distinctions for non-U.S. investor access. There is no claim that this population represents all retail investors.

Candidate consumer products are ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, and Grok. This is an initial coverage list, not a verified popularity ranking. Product weights remain unknown until supported by relevant usage evidence. General app visits, API traffic, and investment-question usage measure different populations and must not be substituted silently.

Every observation belongs to one of three channels:

ChannelInterpretation
Consumer-product observationAn authentic interaction with the named consumer product, with timestamp and observable settings
API proxyA response from a specified provider/model endpoint; consumer routing, memory, browsing, and system instructions may differ
Local proxyA response from a locally hosted model, useful for simulation and engineering but not a measurement of a proprietary product

The first installed pipeline uses the local-proxy channel. Direct consumer-product coverage is zero until actual samples are collected. Having four persona labels or four prompts does not constitute four-model coverage. Having four models does not establish four independent information sources.

3. Event registry and counterfactual design

Each event receives a stable identifier, an information cutoff, a status, alternative branches, and a record of when each fact became available. Scheduled events and unscheduled shocks require different collection protocols.

The initial event family is the 2026 U.S. congressional election. The branches are: unresolved outcome; hypothetical Democratic control of both chambers; hypothetical Republican control of both chambers. These assumptions do not change the presidency and do not imply that a new law has passed. Partial-control branches are a planned extension.

The simulation presents identical investor profiles and evidence packets across branches. Only the event assumption changes. The order is balanced in future larger experiments to separate branch effects from changing model service conditions. The initial daily collector uses deterministic profile rotation and logs the order exactly.

The primary descriptive signal is the change in the recommendation distribution relative to baseline:

Δp(i | e, m, g, t) = p(i | event branch e, model m, profile g, t)
                    − p(i | unresolved baseline, m, g, t).

The unresolved baseline is a conversational control, not an estimate of the market's probability-weighted expectation. Before-event valuation must additionally compare expected demand under possible outcomes with demand the market plausibly anticipates. Those expectations are not observed directly and must remain sensitivity assumptions unless independently estimated.

We preserve cash, broad-market funds, no action, requests for clarification, holds, and sells. A system that requires stock selections in every response would create the concentration it purports to measure.

4. The synthetic investor laboratory

The initial profile bank includes novice investors, ETF-first investors, individual-stock selectors, short-horizon traders, existing holders, loss-averse investors, thematic investors, and European investors with access constraints. Profiles vary capital, horizon, and existing positions. They are experimental conditions, not a recruited sample or a validated demographic model.

Prompts use natural questions such as “Where should I put $5,000?” or “I already own First Solar; should I add, sell, hold, or diversify?” A follow-up asks for a single action while explicitly allowing cash and no change. The initial implementation requests structured answers for reliable extraction. This formatting intervention may alter advice; a future audit must compare it with unconstrained conversation followed by independent extraction.

Sampling is paired across event branches, with repeats measuring response variability. Profiles and wording rotate daily. Within-product repeats are not independent events. If one response fails or truncates, the system records the failure; it does not repeatedly query until it gets an attractive trade.

The initial budgeted workload is four profiles × three branches × two turns, at most 24 local calls per daily run. The commissioning run can use one profile. This is a small prospective instrument, not a statistically representative model of investor behavior. Allocation of further sampling by expected decision value is a planned extension.

5. Recommendation measurement

The runner stores the prompt, raw response object, normalized recommendations, model identifier, source channel, information cutoff, start time, and completion time. Recommendations record action, rank, asset type, optional allocation, and horizon. Unsupported ticker identifiers remain explicitly unverified until matched against a security master.

An initial rank score for a completed conversation is:

a(i) = Σ over recommendations [direction(action) / rank],
direction(buy) = +1; direction(sell) = −1;
direction(hold, avoid, example) = 0.

“Avoid” is not treated as a sell order. The final turn is used for the initial score. Only matched, complete conversations enter branch-minus-baseline comparisons, including zeros for profiles that mention neither side's security. Rank deltas are measured simulation outputs. They are not dollar allocations or expected returns.

For fuller experiments, distinguish recommendation probability, rank prominence, explicit allocation, and actionability rather than collapsing them into one unexamined number. Estimate conditional distributions with shrinkage when cells are sparse. Retain a separate no-action state so total probability remains conserved.

Concentration may be summarized by entropy or a Herfindahl index. Cross-product similarity should also be measured at the source and narrative level: identical tickers supported by identical search results are weaker evidence of independent agreement than distinct information paths. These diagnostics are specified research extensions, not claimed initial results.

6. Demand without a human panel: partial identification

The conceptual flow model is:

F(i,e,h) = Σ over investor groups g
           N(g,e,h) × q(g,e,h) × c(g,e,h) × K(g,e,h) × Δw(i,g,e,h),

where N is event-relevant audience size; q is AI-consultation probability; c is trade-conversion probability; K is incremental deployable capital; and Δw is a change in allocation. Existing holders may sell or reduce positions, creating negative demand.

Without behavioral observation, c and parts of K and Δw remain unknown. Simulating a person saying “I will buy” does not measure any of them. APINA evaluates intervals and alternative joint assumptions. The implemented interval primitive computes extrema over a rectangular assumption set; this is conservative and does not assume independent random variables. More constrained, empirically supported sets may narrow bounds later.

If the admissible conversion interval includes zero, the lower bound on induced buying can be zero. A robust signal should not become confident merely because a model supplies a plausible narrative. When different defensible consumer weights reverse the sign of expected demand, uncertainty must be reported rather than averaged away.

Public search activity, fund flows, turnover, and appropriately sourced aggregate order-flow measures can help test timing and reduced-form predictions. They do not identify AI usage or conversion by themselves. A price rise following both an event and a recommendation is insufficient evidence of causation.

7. The ETF exposure network

Direct stock picks are only one demand route. Advice may concentrate on a fund that holds securities rarely mentioned in conversation. Let B(f,i,t) be the point-in-time weight of security i in fund f:

X(i,t) = D_direct(i,t) + Σ over funds f [B(f,i,t) × D_fund(f,t)].

The implemented exposure primitive conserves total exposure, retains unmapped holdings as a residual, and rejects unsupported recursive fund structures. Holdings must be dated and available at the decision time. The initial collector does not yet maintain a daily holdings feed; an illustrative holdings file must never be mistaken for current observation.

Exposure is not equivalent to immediate constituent execution. ETF shares trade in a secondary market; net creations, inventories, and hedging determine transmission. APINA must test the relationship between recommendation exposure, subsequent net fund flows, and constituent activity before assigning a price-impact coefficient.

For the motivating election scenario, FSLR, ENPH, RUN, SEDG, NXT, and FLNC are research candidates, not current model selections or demonstrated trades. A frozen seed list is used for collecting market context; it is not supplied as a mandatory selection list to the model.

8. From exposure to residual return

Demand relative to ordinary dollar volume is a screening statistic:

pressure(i) = estimated incremental net demand(i) / ordinary dollar volume(i).

Volume is not market depth. Impact depends on spread, depth, volatility, available supply, institutional positioning, and whether the anticipated demand has already been traded. APINA should estimate both continuation and reversal over prespecified horizons.

The target is a feasible residual return:

y(i,e,h) = r(i,e,h) − β_market(i) r_market(e,h)
                     − β_sector(i) r_sector(e,h).

Betas are fitted using pre-event data. Alternative benchmarks are reported because market and sector hedges can overlap and the residual depends on the specification. Entry occurs no earlier than signal completion plus modeled execution delay. Transaction costs include spreads, slippage, fees, financing, and borrow where relevant.

The first pipeline records delayed, unadjusted price snapshots with both market date and observation time. These are sufficient to begin a longitudinal record, not sufficient for reliable intraday backtesting or split/dividend-adjusted return attribution. A point-in-time corporate-action-aware price feed and security master are required before performance claims.

9. Statistical model and what would falsify it

The intended estimator adds recommendation innovations to a strong non-AI baseline:

y(i,e,h) = event effects + pre-event exposures
           + news/attention/momentum controls
           + θ × AI recommendation innovation(i,e)
           + error(i,e,h).

Use regularization and chronological cross-fitting; fit all transformations on training data only. Hold out entire event clusters. Events sharing a news cycle or market regime may need joint clustering. Purge observations with overlapping return windows and embargo labels near train/test boundaries.

The AI component must improve out-of-sample results relative to:

  1. The obvious beneficiary sector or ETF.
  2. News-only event sensitivity.
  3. Attention and momentum without model output.
  4. ETF holdings exposure without model output.
  5. Matched securities with comparable fundamental event exposure.

Evaluate rank correlation, calibrated return intervals, net strategy returns, turnover, drawdown, tail loss, and capacity. Use event-cluster uncertainty, record all tried specifications, and adjust for multiple testing. A large number of prompts from one election cannot supply a large number of independent event observations.

The installed research library provides distribution deltas, ETF exposure accounting, interval demand calculations, descriptive cluster bootstrap, rank correlation, residual-return arithmetic, cost adjustment, robust consumer-mixture checks, and embargo splitting. It does not yet fit or validate the full panel estimator. Advanced mathematical language is not a substitute for a fitted model and held-out evidence.

The primary falsification condition is that AI recommendation innovations fail to add meaningful predictive or economic value to the baselines after realistic timing and costs. A second is that apparent performance disappears under reasonable alternative consumer-mixture and conversion assumptions. The experiment remains informative if either occurs.

10. Adaptive sampling and portfolio research

After a stable baseline is recorded, sampling can be directed toward cells where additional responses may change an actionable conclusion. A starting heuristic is uncertainty reduction × decision sensitivity / query cost. This is an allocation heuristic, not a proven optimal experimental-design policy.

Prospective portfolio research should use a robust objective:

maximize over weights w:
   minimum over plausible parameter sets u [w' μ(u)]
   − risk penalty − execution costs,

subject to gross exposure, event concentration, position size, liquidity, and turnover limits. Hedging choices must be evaluated as part of the strategy rather than added after seeing results. “No trade” is a legitimate optimal decision when the edge is smaller than costs or uncertainty.

There is no brokerage connector and no automated order placement in version 0.1. Portfolio optimization and event-time execution simulation are future modules. This paper reports architecture and initial operations, not an investable track record.

11. Data integrity and provenance

The operational store is SQLite in WAL mode on IMT. At this scale, a transactional embedded database is a better starting point than an unmonitored distributed service. The schema separates runs, documents, prices, experiments, recommendations, budget reservations, operations, artifacts, and backup receipts.

Raw objects are content-addressed by SHA-256. The database records observation time separately from claimed publication or market time. A later scrape of an older article does not make the article available to an earlier decision. Price records retain revisions as distinct observations. Report availability is recorded.

The information cutoff is frozen after collection and before model sampling. Documents collected after that cutoff do not enter the experiment. Daily runs have unique identifiers and preserve failures. A run lock prevents overlap. An interrupted lock requires inspection rather than blind deletion. Source failures result in explicit gaps; no synthetic data fills missing market observations.

Public collection is limited to configured sources, bounded response sizes, rate limits, and robots checks. The initial feeds collect headlines, links, and publication metadata; they do not crawl full articles or evade access restrictions. Untrusted source content is supplied as evidence, never executable instructions. Raw responses and private data stay off the public website.

12. Backups, recovery, and daily operations

The daily Windows task runs on IMT at 00:35 Europe/Istanbul, expressed through an explicit UTC schedule boundary so the machine's local timezone does not silently change the intended instant. Missed runs may execute when available. Local inference depends on IMT and its existing model service being available; source or inference failures remain visible in the report.

Each run creates a consistent database snapshot through SQLite's online backup API, checks database integrity, bundles raw objects and reports, records a per-file SHA-256 manifest, and verifies archive contents. No credential file is included. An archive is not considered remotely backed up merely because it exists on IMT.

A daily Codex follow-up transfers the latest verified archive to the dedicated private APINA CAPITAL Google Drive folder, verifies returned metadata and checksum when available, and records a backup receipt on IMT. The follow-up depends on the desktop automation environment being available. Remote upload failures must be reported and retried without deleting the local copy. A full restore should be rehearsed periodically; archive creation alone is insufficient evidence of recoverability.

Daily operational evidence includes last successful collection, completed versus failed experiments, local/consumer/API coverage, source gaps, latest verified snapshot, remote backup receipt, and accounted API spend. Public publication contains only deliberately selected research material and non-sensitive status.

13. Compute and the $300 constraint

IMT's RTX 5090 performs local inference through the existing Qwen service. Requests are sequential, with call and wall-clock limits. APINA does not unload another job's model or change the GPU service configuration. Local inference avoids marginal API charges but still consumes electricity, hardware time, and opportunity cost.

The user authorized up to $300 of OpenAI credit. Credit available within Codex is treated as development/research capacity unless separately usable API access is established. The installed runtime has no discovered dedicated OpenAI API credential and paid calls are disabled by default.

For a future dedicated API configuration, the runner reserves conservative request cost atomically before dispatch, applies daily and lifetime project limits, limits output, disables paid tools, and reconciles returned token usage. Ambiguous failures retain the reservation and are not automatically retried. Pricing must be explicitly configured and verified within seven days before a paid call. The ceiling applies to APINA's ledger, not unrelated usage on a shared account. Provider billing remains the authority.[4–5]

No OpenAI model is assumed to reproduce another vendor's actual consumer behavior. Purchasing OpenAI inference alone cannot create authentic observations of three other products.

14. Prospective protocol

The commissioning sequence is:

  1. Verify the local database, scheduler, GPU inference, snapshot, restore, and Drive upload.
  2. Collect a stable baseline before the event; preserve actual product and model coverage.
  3. Freeze the event definition, targets, horizons, baselines, and first analysis before examining outcomes.
  4. Add authentic consumer/API observations only when access and provenance are established.
  5. Accumulate additional, independently classified event families rather than retuning around one election.
  6. Fit the first model only with point-in-time prices, corporate-action treatment, sufficient events, and recorded alternative specifications.
  7. Paper trade using feasible entries and realistic costs; report negative findings.

Historical model replays can debug the pipeline but cannot establish clean prospective results when the model may know subsequent events. Instructions to “pretend it is last year” do not remove that knowledge. The primary evidence must come from timestamped forward collection.

The ambition is a rigorous event-and-attention research operation. The moat, if one develops, is a longitudinal record of what advice was actually available, how it changed, and what markets did afterward. Query volume and clever prose are not substitutes for that record.
