About

Product thinking meets global development

Petri Mikael Autio is the product leader behind mWater and Solstice. mWater is the world's most widely used open data management platform for water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH). Solstice is its sector-agnostic sibling, extending the same technology to health, education, agriculture, and other domains in low-resource settings, and Juno, the newest sister platform, extends it further into ecological and environmental monitoring. Since 2018, Petri has shipped 300+ major features across these platforms, serving governments, multilateral organizations, and community-based utilities on every continent.

His work combines technical product management with hands-on understanding of what it takes to deliver services in low-resource, low-connectivity environments. The features he ships address real constraints: offline functionality in rural Malawi, multi-language surveys for Ethiopian government programs, geospatial analysis for Ugandan water utilities, AI-powered translations and integrations, and encryption at rest for government security requirements.

These platforms underpin national WASH monitoring systems, large-scale donor programs, and frontline service delivery. His product decisions, from the Water Supply Planning Tool to workflows automation to 28 portal languages, affect how millions of people access clean water and sanitation. In 2026 he authored mWater's public AI strategy, one of the first from any WASH data platform, and represented the mWater Foundation at the UN AI for Good Global Summit in Geneva.

AI Leadership

Setting the AI agenda for WASH data

In July 2026, Petri authored Building for the Future: An AI Strategy for WASH Data (also on the mWater blog), one of the first published AI strategies from any WASH data platform. It is not a vision paper: every pillar is backed by features already shipped and in daily use across 198 countries and territories.

"Design features that get better whenever the underlying models get better."
The core design principle of the mWater AI strategy

A Published, Public Strategy

The strategy stakes out how AI should serve frontline water and sanitation work: as a capacity-multiplier for local professionals, not a new dependency on consultants.

  • Assume AI agents will find your APIs, so build sanctioned, structured access
  • Separate data from structure to ringfence high-risk elements like survey schemas
  • Make documentation machine-readable
  • Keep humans in control with approval workflows and point-in-time recovery
  • View the interactive AI Roadmap

Shipped AI, Not Slideware

A steady cadence of AI features, each delivered free to all users:

  • AI dashboard translation, November 2024
  • AI survey translation, January 2025
  • The Integrator: AI builds data import/export integrations from plain language, funded by UNICEF Madagascar, February 2026
  • AI-powered widget design: describe charts, pivots, and maps conversationally, April 2026
  • MCP server: connect Claude, Cursor, or Codex to query data and docs and propose changes, June 2026

Responsible by Design

AI capability paired with governance, so governments and NGOs can adopt it with confidence:

  • Every AI-proposed data change requires human review and approval
  • AI-generated integration code runs sandboxed, with encrypted credential storage
  • Permission-aware access: AI assistants see only what the user can see
  • Data sovereignty commitments, presented in mWater's webinar on data ownership in the age of cloud and AI
  • No proprietary fine-tuned models: features improve as foundation models improve
