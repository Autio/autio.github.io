01 / Platform product leadership

mWater: evolving a global platform for water and sanitation

Since 2018, I have led product development at mWater, working with our team and partners on data collection, analysis, asset management, workflows and integrations. The same platform must serve field staff, utilities and national governments, often in different languages and with unreliable connectivity.

mWater describes itself as the most widely used data platform in the WASH sector. By November 2026 it had more than 400,000 registered users across 200 countries and territories. As of September 2026, the platform had recorded 52 million survey responses. These are figures for the platform as a whole.

Registration and reach: November 2026. Survey responses: September 2026. See mWater impact figures. Platform description: mWater.

02 / Product launch and growth

Solstice: from an idea to adoption across sectors

When I joined mWater in 2018, Solstice was an idea for extending the platform beyond water and sanitation. I took it through positioning, app and portal deployment, and the management of staff, projects and partner relationships.

At Medair, I encouraged the WASH team to use the Solstice identity so colleagues in other departments could adopt the system. Its use subsequently expanded into health and other areas.

By September 2026, Solstice had grown to more than 42,000 registered users across 155 countries. At that point, Solstice accounted for approximately one in five registrations across mWater and Solstice during 2026.

03 / AI access

A useful way in for AI agents

Users wanted their AI assistants to work with mWater data. We needed to provide that access without weakening permissions or losing human oversight.

My role
Product leadership and the platform’s published AI strategy.
Design choice
Provide permission-aware access for agents, require human review of proposed changes and cap subsidised usage.
In practice
Built-in features support data analysis. The MCP server gives assistants access to data the user is permitted to see. The Integrator helps practitioners connect external systems in plain language.

From a reported fault to a repair

A reported fault is only useful if someone can take responsibility, repair it and record the outcome.

My role

Independent AI safety engineering · Experimental language

Hegelese: checking what an AI rewrite preserves

Hegelese is an experimental functional language and finite-process checker. It tests which behaviours survive a proposed change to a program and which distinctions are lost.

When a preservation claim fails, the checker can return a counterexample. The bootstrap interpreter and finite-process checker run today. Static typing, a compiler and general proof checking are not yet implemented.
